Munshi

Handwritten medical documents in Hindi and English become a claim packet an insurer will accept. When the models disagree, it stops.

Built withPiyush Rane
WhereSarvam × GrowthX Epoch Buildathon, 26 July 2026
The design ideaTwo-model agreement, with abstention on disagreement
Codegithub.com/SuryanshD/munshi

The problem

Filing a health-insurance claim in India means assembling paperwork that was written by hand, often in a mix of Hindi and English, sometimes both inside one line. Doctors' handwriting is the joke that it is. The forms have a required shape, and a packet that's missing a field or has the wrong figure in one comes back rejected, which for the person filing it means weeks.

The errors here are not symmetric. A blank field is an inconvenience. A confidently wrong number on a medical claim is a rejection, or a claim settled for the wrong amount.

The decision the whole thing turns on

Two models have to agree before the system commits to a reading. When they disagree, it abstains and flags the field.

Most extraction pipelines return a best guess with a confidence score, and a model will report high confidence on a figure it invented. So rather than asking one model how sure it is, we ask two models independently and use their agreement as the signal.

Disagreement is a genuinely useful signal because it correlates with the thing you actually care about: illegibility and ambiguity. When two models reading the same smudged number produce different answers, that number is probably not legible, and the right output is to say so rather than pick one.

Decisions, and what each one cost

Agreement instead of self-reported confidence

Two independent reads, compared, rather than one read with a confidence number.

cost Roughly double the inference for every document. For a claim, that's obviously worth it. For a high-volume low-stakes pipeline it wouldn't be.

Abstaining rather than filling the field

The system will hand back an incomplete packet instead of a complete guess.

cost It looks worse in a demo, because a full output reads as success and a partial one reads as failure. It's the right behaviour anyway, and the flagged fields tell a human exactly where to look.

Committing only what survives the check

What both reads agree on goes into the packet. What they do not is left for a human.

cost The packet a human receives can be incomplete, so the flags have to be clear enough to act on without re-reading the whole document.

Why bilingual matters more than it sounds

Real documents mix scripts inside a single line: a Devanagari word, a Latin drug name and a numeral. Anything that picks one language for the whole page will misread the parts that are not in it.

It was built at the Sarvam × GrowthX Epoch Buildathon. Documents like these are what break any tool that assumes monolingual input.

What I'd change

We have no measured abstention rate. The design is right and I'd defend it, but the number that decides whether it's useful is what fraction of fields it refuses on a real stack of documents. Too high and a human is doing the work anyway, which means the tool has just added a step. Getting that number is the first thing I'd do with more than a buildathon's worth of time, and it needs a labelled set of real claims to measure against.
