DRDO, twice

DEAL, Dehradun: MAC protocols in tactical MANETs (Jun–Jul 2023)

I was a summer trainee in the IP Based Protocols Group at DEAL, a DRDO lab. The subject was medium-access control in tactical mobile ad-hoc networks.

A tactical MANET has no fixed infrastructure. Every node is mobile and every node routes, and the topology changes while traffic is flowing. There's no base station to arbitrate who transmits, so the medium-access protocol is doing that job, and its behaviour under contention is what determines whether the network stays usable when nodes move or drop out.

I built simulated network environments in NS2 and compared MAC protocols under varying conditions, then delivered a technical report with 10 recommendations to the group. Worth being precise: it was a simulation study and nothing was deployed. It was also my first exposure to systems whose failure mode is degradation under load rather than a crash. Most of what I work on now fails the same way.

IRDE: electronic circuit design (Jun–Aug 2022)

The summer before, at IRDE, another DRDO lab, I worked on electronic circuit design and logic gates. It's the least fashionable line on my CV and I'd keep it. Working out what a circuit does at the gate level is the same kind of reasoning I now do on systems.

IIT Delhi: fault detection in fuel cells (2023)

A research summer in Dr. Anil Verma's lab, on "Fault Detection and Diagnosis in PEMFC using AI", part of the lab's wider sustainable-energy research.

A proton-exchange-membrane fuel cell degrades in ways that show up in its electrical behaviour before they show up as failure. Flooding, membrane drying and gas-supply faults each leave a signature, and separating those signatures from ordinary variation is the diagnosis problem. It meant applying AI methods to a physical system while it was operating, which is a different exercise from a clean dataset: the labels come from the physics, and being wrong has a mechanical consequence rather than a metric one.

I was two years into a B.Tech at UPES when I got the seat.

IBM India: plant-disease detection (Jun–Jul 2024)

I led a plant-disease detection and prediction project to 92% accuracy, working with agricultural experts to refine the model, alongside NLP research.

The accuracy figure is the least useful thing about it. The work that mattered was done with the agricultural experts, refining what the model was being asked to tell apart.

Glance: product analytics (Jun–Aug 2024)

A year before I joined InMobi as an engineer, I spent a summer at Glance as a product analyst. I built and maintained 4 Superset dashboards carrying 50+ charts across the USA and Singapore clusters, including a product-alert dashboard that surfaced daily engagement deviations by OEM and region, and ran regional deep dives across 30+ engagement metrics into a bi-weekly report for product managers. I also did market research on state demographics and smartphone usage for Xiaomi, Realme and Samsung.

The transferable part was learning what a metric is for. I built a few dashboards nobody made a decision from before I built useful ones.

The published article

As a student I published "Use of AI and Its Benefits on Quality Assurance (QA)" in the official journal of the DGQA, the Directorate General of Quality Assurance under India's Ministry of Defence. It's DGQA's own departmental publication, with a circulation of 5,000+.

The subject was AI in the quality-assurance loop. Years later I shipped something with that shape: a campaign-compliance path where a model clears what it can and a human sees the rest.
